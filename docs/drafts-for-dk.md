## FAQ answer 1: Is it a lack of faith if I am still ill?

> No. My Christian faith shapes how I see purpose, dignity, truth and stewardship. It is how I listen, and it is why I believe people deserve better. In many of the communities I love, including African and faith communities, chronic illness gets minimised. Prayer is offered where practical support is also needed, and staying busy becomes proof that you are fine. Being ill is not a verdict on your faith. It is a reason to be heard, and supported, properly.

Locked: sentences two to five (Advocacy and Faith, About). New: "No." and the last two sentences.
Links: Advocacy and Faith.

## FAQ answer 2: How can a church support someone with an invisible illness?

> Some things get harder because we are expected to hide them. An illness nobody can see. A diagnosis that came late. A prayer that has not been answered. Prayer is offered where practical support is also needed, so offer both in the same breath. Ask how someone is, then ask again until the true answer comes out. Make room for the unsaid: chronic illness, mental health, unanswered prayer, the things many of us carry into church and never say out loud. If you would like this conversation in your church, invite me.

Locked: the first four sentences (Advocacy and Faith), "Prayer is offered where practical support is also needed" (About), "until the true answer comes out" (HAWFA), the list in the unsaid sentence (The Unsaid). New: the joining phrases and the last sentence.
Links: Advocacy and Faith, Speaking enquiry form.

## FAQ answer 3: What should I say to my employer about an invisible illness?

> Say what you need, not what you have. Describe the cost rather than the symptom, and offer the shape of the help you need, so it is easy to say yes. Words for Asking for Help gives you clear words for asking for help, setting limits and explaining needs other people cannot see, without having to defend every request. If you want this for a whole team, I speak on being heard inside the system: long-term illness, neurodiversity and finding the words to ask for what you need.

Locked: the third sentence (Words for Asking for Help page intro) and the fourth (Speaking topics). New: the first two sentences, which also appear in the essay draft on asking for help.
Links: Words for Asking for Help, Speaking topics.

## FAQ answer 4: Is PACE like spoon theory?

> They solve different problems. Spoon theory gives you a way to describe limited energy to other people. PACE is how I plan work around the capacity you really have. The PACE Energy Check asks four quick questions about your body, energy and mind, and gives you the pace your day can hold. The PACE Week Planner lets you choose your capacity for the week first, then up to three focus tasks that can move between days. Use spoon theory to explain. Use PACE to plan.

Locked: the three PACE sentences (FAQ, Toolkit). New: the spoon theory sentences and the last two lines.
Links: PACE Energy Check, PACE Week Planner.
